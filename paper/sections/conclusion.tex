\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluated a leakage-controlled framework combining RFECV with heterogeneous stacking for prediction of the PIMA diabetes outcome. Repeated cross-validation showed that the common random-forest-ranked RFECV reduced the predictor set by about a quarter without materially changing discrimination. Under the prespecified hyperparameters, stacking did not significantly improve ROC-AUC over regularised logistic regression (0.843 versus 0.842). The stacking model provided sensitivity-oriented operating points, with the highest observed hold-out sensitivity (0.815) and MCC (0.532) on this split. However, its class-weighted probabilities were systematically miscalibrated and should not be interpreted as absolute risks without recalibration. Threshold and decision-curve analyses further indicated that the choice of operating threshold may be more consequential than the choice between stacking and logistic regression. Because the predictors include 2-h OGTT values and the outcome is a historical WHO-criteria definition, these findings are a methodological benchmark of internal validity on the PIMA cohort, not a clinically validated model, and require external validation before any clinical application.
