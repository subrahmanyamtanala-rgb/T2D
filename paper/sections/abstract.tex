\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background:} Machine-learning models for type~2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) risk prediction often report optimistic performance because imputation or feature selection is fitted before validation, and rarely test differences between models.

\noindent\textbf{Methods:} A leakage-controlled pipeline combined median imputation, eight deterministic derived features, recursive feature elimination with cross-validation (RFECV) and an eight-learner stacking ensemble, with every step re-fitted inside each validation split. On the PIMA Indians Diabetes dataset (768 women, 268 with diabetes), 11 learners with prespecified hyperparameters (eight base learners, two references, the stack) were compared with and without a common random-forest-ranked RFECV subset, using 5$\times$10-fold repeated cross-validation with corrected confidence intervals and Holm-adjusted tests. A 154-patient hold-out set used bootstrap intervals and DeLong tests. We also assessed calibration, sensitivity-targeted thresholds, decision curves, imputation and SHAP attributions.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} RFECV cut inputs by 25\% without loss of discrimination, keeping a stable core of glucose, BMI, the diabetes pedigree function and their interactions. Cross-validated ROC-AUC was 0.843 (95\% CI 0.809--0.878) for stacking + RFECV and 0.842 (0.809--0.875) for logistic regression (adjusted $p = 1.00$). On the hold-out set, stacking + RFECV reached sensitivity 0.815, specificity 0.740, MCC 0.532 and ROC-AUC 0.822; no ROC-AUC difference was significant. Predicted probabilities were systematically too high (calibration intercept $-0.62$, slope 1.00), consistent with balanced class weighting. Alternative imputation changed nothing.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} Under leakage-free validation, added ensemble complexity gave no discrimination gain over logistic regression, whereas calibration and threshold choice mattered more. As a methodological benchmark on a cohort whose predictors include 2-h OGTT values, these internal results do not establish clinical transportability.
\end{abstract}
