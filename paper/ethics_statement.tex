{\Large\bfseries Ethics Statement}

\textbf{Manuscript title:} Leakage-controlled evaluation of stacking and recursive feature elimination for diabetes outcome prediction on the PIMA dataset

\textbf{Journal:} Computers in Biology and Medicine

\bigskip
\textbf{Human participants and data.} This study is a secondary analysis of the publicly available PIMA Indians Diabetes Database, originally collected by the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases and distributed through the UCI Machine Learning Repository. The dataset contains 768 records with eight clinical measurements and a binary outcome. It contains no names, dates, locations or other direct identifiers. The author had no access to identifiable information and no contact with participants.

\textbf{Ethical approval.} No new data were collected from human participants, no intervention was performed, and only de-identified public data were analysed. Ethical approval was therefore not required for this study.

\textbf{Informed consent.} Not applicable. The study used de-identified, publicly available secondary data, and no individual can be identified from the data or from the results reported.

\textbf{Animal research.} Not applicable. No animals were involved.

\textbf{Data use.} The dataset was used solely for methodological research, consistent with its public distribution for research purposes. The exact file used, its source and its checksum are documented in the supplementary material, and all code is publicly available.

\textbf{Responsible reporting.} Because the dataset represents a single historical cohort of women of Pima heritage, the manuscript explicitly states that the results are an internal methodological benchmark and must not be used for clinical decisions without external validation.
