Background: Machine-learning models for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) risk prediction often report optimistic performance because preprocessing or feature selection is fitted before validation.

Methods: A leakage-controlled pipeline combined median imputation, eight derived features, recursive feature elimination with cross-validation (RFECV) and an eight-learner stacking ensemble, with every step re-fitted inside each validation split. On the PIMA Indians Diabetes dataset (768 women, 268 with diabetes), 11 learners with prespecified hyperparameters were compared with and without a common random-forest-ranked RFECV subset, using 5x10-fold repeated cross-validation with corrected confidence intervals and Holm-adjusted tests. A 154-patient hold-out set used bootstrap intervals and DeLong tests. Calibration, thresholds, decision curves, imputation and SHAP attributions were also assessed.

Results: RFECV cut inputs by 25% without loss of discrimination, keeping a stable core of glucose, BMI, the diabetes pedigree function and their interactions. Cross-validated ROC-AUC was 0.843 (95% CI 0.809-0.878) for stacking + RFECV and 0.842 (0.809-0.875) for logistic regression (adjusted p = 1.00). On the hold-out set, stacking + RFECV reached sensitivity 0.815, specificity 0.740, MCC 0.532 and ROC-AUC 0.822; no ROC-AUC difference was significant. Predicted probabilities were systematically too high (calibration intercept -0.62, slope 1.00), consistent with balanced class weighting.

Conclusions: Under leakage-free validation, ensemble complexity gave no discrimination gain over logistic regression, whereas calibration and threshold choice mattered more. These internal results, from a cohort whose predictors include 2-h OGTT values, do not establish clinical transportability.
