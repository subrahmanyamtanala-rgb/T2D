Background: Machine-learning models for type 2 diabetes risk prediction often report optimistic performance because preprocessing or feature selection is fitted before validation.

Methods: A leakage-controlled pipeline combined median imputation, derived features, recursive feature elimination with cross-validation (RFECV) and an eight-learner stacking ensemble, with every step re-fitted inside each validation split. On the PIMA Indians Diabetes dataset (768 women, 268 with diabetes), 11 learners with prespecified hyperparameters were compared with and without RFECV using 5x10-fold repeated cross-validation with corrected confidence intervals and Holm-adjusted tests, plus a 154-patient hold-out set with DeLong tests. Calibration, thresholds, decision curves and SHAP attributions were also assessed. The framework was replicated in 1815 NHANES 2013-2014 adults with an HbA1c-defined outcome.

Results: RFECV cut inputs by 25% without loss of discrimination. Cross-validated ROC-AUC was 0.843 (95% CI 0.809-0.878) for stacking + RFECV and 0.842 (0.809-0.875) for logistic regression (adjusted p = 1.00). On the hold-out set, stacking + RFECV reached sensitivity 0.815, specificity 0.740 and ROC-AUC 0.822; no ROC-AUC difference was significant. Predicted probabilities were systematically too high (calibration intercept -0.62, slope 1.00), consistent with balanced class weighting. In NHANES, logistic regression again matched stacking (0.787 versus 0.785) and the calibration offset recurred (-0.82).

Conclusions: Under leakage-free validation, ensemble complexity gave no discrimination gain over logistic regression in two cohorts, whereas calibration and threshold choice mattered more. These internal results do not establish clinical transportability.
